01 · AI + PROPERTY TECHNOLOGY
EnvironmentMultifamily property technology SaaS
My roleSenior Business Analyst supporting discovery through delivery
FocusAI renter engagement, supported behavior, escalation and handoff

AI Leasing Assistant

How do you automate renter conversations without letting the AI confidently handle something it shouldn't?

01 · ProblemRecurring renter inquiries consume leasing-team time — but not every conversation is safe to automate.
02 · My roleDefine supported behavior, missing context, handoff and edge cases before delivery.
03 · ApproachModel the conversation around three outcomes: answer, clarify or escalate.
04 · OutcomeClearer AI boundaries translated into stories, AC and backlog decisions.
It wasn’t an answer-generation problem. It was a boundary problem.

The harder question was not what the AI could say. It was when it should stop trying and let a person take over.

The friction

The request sounded simple: answer more questions automatically.

The actual work was figuring out where automation helped, where it created risk, and what the renter should experience when the AI reached its limit.

Some questions need judgment.

Not every inquiry should be treated like a standard FAQ.

Missing context changes the answer.

A supported intent may still need clarification before a safe response is possible.

Escalation is part of the product.

Handoff needs triggers, context preservation and a clear next state.

Confidence is not correctness.

Low-confidence behavior has to be designed explicitly.

How I broke it down

Three normal outcomes instead of one happy path.

ANSWERSupported intent + enough context
↓
CLARIFYSupported intent + missing information
↓
ESCALATEUnsupported / sensitive / low confidence / human requested
Key decisions

The decisions that changed the requirements.

DECISION 01

Treat escalation as a first-class journey.

That introduced explicit requirements for trigger conditions, conversation context, user messaging and post-handoff behavior.

DECISION 02

Allow clarification, but don’t let the assistant keep guessing.

Clarification can recover missing context; repeated guessing increases risk and frustration.

Looking backEscalation looked like an exception at first. It really needed to be part of the main product journey.

That shift exposed requirements around context, ownership and user messaging much earlier.

02 · ENTERPRISE SAAS + INTEGRATION
EnvironmentEnterprise property-management SaaS
Product areaLeasing & Rents
FocusLease execution, external e-signature service, statuses and exceptions

E-Signature Integration

What really happens between “send for signature” and “lease completed”?

01 · ProblemOne visible action crossed multiple systems, data states and ownership boundaries.
02 · My roleMake the handoff explicit: what gets sent, returned and owned.
03 · ApproachModel status, mapping and failure paths together — not just the API happy path.
04 · OutcomeA testable end-to-end workflow including void, resend and callback failure.
It wasn’t a “send document” problem. It was a state-management problem.

The product had to remain understandable while the lease moved between internal and external states.

Target journey

The happy path still needed ownership at every step.

Lease generatedInternal lease record is authoritative.
↓
Validate document + signer dataCatch missing or invalid inputs before send.
↓
Create e-signature requestExternal request now has its own state.
↓
Resident(s) signPartial completion and multiple signers matter.
↓
External status returnsCallbacks must map back to product language.
↓
Lease updatesStatus, completed document and history remain traceable.
Key decisions

Exception behavior mattered as much as the success path.

DECISION 01

Map internal and external statuses explicitly.

Provider terminology should not leak directly into the product experience.

DECISION 02

Define what happens if the lease changes after send.

The active signature request has to stay aligned with the authoritative lease.

Looking backThe visible feature was small. The state model behind it was not.

The strongest requirements came from treating data, status, ownership and exceptions as one product problem.

03 · EDTECH + MODERNIZATION
EnvironmentEstablished K–12 learning platform
My roleSenior Business Analyst on the delivery team
FocusLegacy behavior, redesign gaps, requirements and UAT

Modernizing an Established LMS

How do you redesign a mature product without accidentally removing the reasons it still works?

01 · ProblemNew designs looked cleaner, but some old behavior represented real business rules.
02 · My roleSeparate required behavior from historical UI constraints.
03 · ApproachCompare legacy behavior, intended design and business rules.
04 · OutcomeMore delivery-ready stories with clearer permissions, validations and UAT coverage.
It wasn’t a redesign problem. It was a behavior-preservation problem.

A cleaner screen is not an improvement if it quietly removes safeguards, permissions or workflow rules the business still depends on.

How I analyzed the redesign

Every meaningful difference became a decision.

Existing behaviorDecisionRequirement impact
Action limited to certain rolesPreserveInclude permission acceptance criteria.
Multi-page legacy navigationRedesignConsolidate if the underlying behavior remains intact.
Warning before destructive actionPreserve intentUX can change; safeguard cannot disappear.
Legacy field absent from new designClarifyOpen question before the story is ready.
Looking backLegacy UI is not the same thing as legacy requirement.

The useful work was deciding which behaviors were true rules, which were historical constraints, and which were still unresolved.

04 · PLATFORM MIGRATION
Project typeBackground-screening provider replacement
Business journeyApplicant screening / leasing
FocusCurrent/future state, cutover, migration and decommissioning

Replacing a Background Check Platform

How do you replace a vendor when transactions are still moving through the system you’re trying to retire?

01 · ProblemNew requests could move to a replacement, but history and in-flight work still depended on the old provider.
02 · My roleMap what would break if the old platform disappeared too early.
03 · ApproachModel current state, future state and cutover separately.
04 · OutcomeA more realistic migration and decommissioning plan with explicit transition scenarios.
It wasn’t a vendor-switch problem. It was a transition-state problem.

The hardest scenario was the work already in motion when the new platform went live.

Critical scenario

A screening starts before cutover and finishes after cutover.

That one case forces decisions about ownership, callbacks, history, support access and when the old platform can truly be retired.

AreaCurrent stateFuture-state question
Screening requestLegacy providerWhat triggers the new provider after cutover?
StatusLegacy modelHow do new provider states map to product language?
HistoryPast results in old platformWhat must be migrated, retained or remain accessible?
SupportLegacy procedures and credentialsWhat access remains until in-flight work is closed?
Looking backThe old system is not gone just because new transactions stop entering it.

Decommissioning only becomes real when in-flight work, reporting, access, support and retention all have an exit path.

05 · AI-ASSISTED BA
ProblemStories can reach refinement with hidden assumptions or incomplete context
ToolingClaude + product / delivery context
BoundaryAI assists analysis; BA/Product retains judgment

BA Pre-Flight Workflow

A well-written user story can still be the wrong requirement.

01 · ProblemDraft quality can hide requirement uncertainty.
02 · My roleDesign a repeatable readiness check before refinement.
03 · ApproachUse AI to pressure-test facts vs assumptions, states, permissions and dependencies.
04 · OutcomeA workflow that can legitimately say “not ready” and explain why.
It wasn’t a story-writing problem. It was a requirements-readiness problem.

Claude can draft excellent acceptance criteria around an assumption. The value is exposing that assumption before it hardens into a requirement.

The pre-flight

Before writing the story, ask whether we understand the problem.

Raw request / notes / design / existing ticket
↓
Extract goal, actors, behavior and stated rules
↓
Separate confirmed information from assumptions
↓
Surface missing states, permissions, validations and dependencies
↓
Open questions → draft story + AC from confirmed behavior → BA review
Worked example

“Refund handling is the same as today” is not a requirement yet.

SourceWhat pre-flight catchesDecision
“Users should be able to cancel a booking.”Which users? Which statuses?Clarify permission and eligibility.
“Not once it has started.”What defines “started”?Define the state/event explicitly.
“Refund handling is the same as today.”AI may assume automatic refund.Reject unsupported assumption; research current behavior.
Looking backThe useful output is not always a story. Sometimes it is a better question.

The workflow is valuable precisely because it can conclude that a requirement is not ready yet.

Estimator Aide

Why does estimation sometimes start with Engineering rediscovering context we already have?

01 · ProblemRefinement can start from a blank page while useful historical context is scattered.
02 · My roleBring better evidence into the conversation without estimating for Engineering.
03 · ApproachCombine comparable delivery history with code-analysis signals and caveats.
04 · OutcomeA pre-estimation brief with comparable work, likely touchpoints and questions.
It wasn’t an estimation problem. It was a context problem.

The goal is not for AI to assign points. It is to help Engineering start from evidence instead of a blank sheet.

Estimator Aide workflow

Evidence first. Estimate second.

Proposed story / requirement
↓
Understand functional scope and behavior
↓
Find genuinely comparable Azure DevOps tickets
↓
Review likely code areas, dependencies and regression signals
↓
Summarize evidence + caveats + questions
↓
Engineering validates evidence and owns final sizing
DELIBERATELY DOES

Surface evidence and uncertainty.

Comparable tickets with caveats, likely touchpoints, dependency signals and questions.

DELIBERATELY DOES NOT

Turn historical work into an automatic estimate.

No automatic story points, fake equivalence or overriding Engineering judgment.

Looking backThe most useful output may be “no genuinely comparable ticket found.”

That is better than manufacturing confidence. Evidence quality matters more than producing a neat answer.

How I work

The patterns behind the case studies.

I tend to be most useful when the request looks straightforward but the underlying behavior is not.

I look for the rules behind the request.

Stakeholders usually describe the change they want, not every condition around it.

I bring edge cases in early.

Exceptions expose missing permissions, states, dependencies and business rules.

I make trade-offs visible.

A requirement is stronger when the team understands what was considered and why a path was chosen.

I write for Product, Engineering and QA together.

If the same story can reasonably be interpreted three different ways, it still needs work.
