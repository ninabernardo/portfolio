Case 01 · AI product
Environment Property technology / multifamily SaaS
My role Senior Business Analyst supporting discovery through delivery
Focus AI renter engagement, leasing workflow, escalation behaviour

AI Leasing Assistant

The objective was an AI layer that could handle common renter inquiries while recognising when a leasing professional needed to step in. My first step was to define those boundaries: what the AI could handle on its own, when it should ask for more information, and when it had to hand off.

The harder part was not teaching the AI what to answer. It was defining when it should stop trying.
What I needed to understand

Where could automation help without creating a bad renter experience?

Renter inquiries can arrive outside office hours, but not every conversation is safe or useful to automate. I broke the experience into supported intents, clarification paths, unsupported requests, and human handoff conditions.

My analysis

I treated escalation as part of the normal journey.

That meant the requirements had to cover when escalation happens, what context follows the conversation, what the prospect sees, and what happens if no leasing professional is immediately available.

Simplified conversation flow
Prospect sends inquiry
↓
Identify intent + available context
↓
Confident supported intent → respond
↓
Missing information → clarify
↓
Unsupported / sensitive / human requested → escalate with context
Representative requirements
  • The assistant must identify the user's likely intent before selecting a response path.
  • Low-confidence or unsupported requests must not be presented as confidently resolved.
  • A prospect asking for a person must be able to leave the automated path.
  • Relevant conversation context must be preserved during handoff.
  • Interactions and escalation outcomes must be available for review.
Edge cases I would explicitly cover
Scenario Why it matters Requirement direction
Prospect explicitly asks for a person The system should not trap the user in automation. Escalate immediately and preserve context.
Question may expose fair-housing risk A fluent AI response can still be inappropriate. Use approved response handling or human escalation.
Same prospect contacts through multiple channels Duplicate or conflicting conversations create confusion. Use available identity/context signals before starting a new journey.
No agent is available after escalation Escalation still needs a complete user experience. Confirm follow-up expectations and retain conversation context.
MVP decision

What I would keep in the first release

  • Supported FAQ intents
  • Clarification for missing information
  • Human escalation with context
  • Interaction history
Deferred

What I would not rush into MVP

  • Autonomous high-risk leasing actions
  • Deep personalization without enough evidence or controls
  • Behaviour that depends on data the assistant cannot reliably verify
My contribution

I translated an AI product objective into explicit behavioural boundaries, conversation paths, escalation conditions, representative requirements, acceptance criteria, and edge cases. I also used those boundaries to separate what was safe for MVP from capabilities that needed more evidence first.

Case 02 · Integration
Environment Enterprise property-management SaaS
Product area Leasing & Rents
Focus Lease execution, e-signature integration, downstream status handling

E-Signature Integration for Leasing

A leasing workflow can look complete inside the product while still depending on another platform to finish the transaction. The BA work is making the handoff between those systems explicit — what gets sent, what comes back, what each status means, and what happens when the happy path breaks.

End-to-end workflow
Lease is generated in Leasing & Rents
↓
Validate document + signer information
↓
Create e-signature envelope / request
↓
Resident(s) review and sign
↓
E-signature platform returns status
↓
Leasing & Rents updates lease status and makes completed document available
Questions I would drive in discovery
  • Which system owns each piece of signer and lease data?
  • Who can initiate, void, or resend a signature request?
  • How are multiple signers handled?
  • What happens if the lease changes after it is sent?
  • How does the product recover if the downstream platform succeeds but the callback/update fails?
  • How are duplicate envelopes prevented?
Representative field mapping
Leasing data E-signature concept
Lease / transaction IDReference / envelope metadata
Resident nameSigner name
Resident emailSigner delivery address
Generated lease documentDocument to be signed
Representative status mapping
External status Product status User experience
Sent / Delivered Awaiting signatures User can see who is still outstanding.
Completed Signed Completed document is available and workflow can continue.
Declined Signature declined Leasing user can review the reason and decide next action.
Voided / Expired No longer active User needs a clear path to generate or send a new request.
Exception scenario

What if the lease changes after it has already been sent for signature?

This is where the integration becomes a product problem, not just an API problem. The workflow needs an explicit rule for whether the existing envelope remains valid, whether it must be voided, what the resident sees, and how the revised lease is generated and resent.

Representative requirement direction: a materially revised lease should not silently continue under an existing signature request. The user needs a controlled path to cancel/void the old request and send the updated document.
My contribution

This kind of work required me to look beyond the screen-level requirement and understand the full transaction across systems. I would document the trigger, data mapping, statuses, permissions, failure paths, retries, and user-facing behaviour so Engineering and QA were not left to infer integration rules during delivery.

Case 03 · Modernization
Environment K–12 learning platform / SaaS
My role Senior Business Analyst on the delivery team
Focus Legacy behaviour, redesign, requirements, acceptance criteria, UAT

Modernizing an Established LMS

Modernization is not a clean-sheet redesign. Existing screens often contain years of business rules, exceptions, and user expectations that are easy to miss when the conversation starts with a new design. My role is to help separate behaviour that must be preserved from behaviour that exists only because the old experience worked that way.

A redesigned screen can look simpler while accidentally removing behaviour the business still depends on.
How I approach it

I compare the existing product, the intended design, and the business rules behind both.

I look for missing states, permissions, validations, dependencies, and actions before a design is treated as development-ready.

What I am trying to avoid

Rebuilding legacy behaviour blindly — or losing it accidentally.

Some behaviours are genuine requirements. Others are historical workarounds or UI limitations. The important part is making that distinction explicit.

Modernization analysis
Existing behaviour What I found Decision Requirement impact
Action is available only to certain users This is a real permission rule, not just an old UI choice. Preserve Include role/permission acceptance criteria.
User must navigate through multiple pages to complete one task Navigation reflects legacy page structure rather than a business rule. Redesign Consolidate the workflow where possible.
System warns before a destructive action Warning protects against irreversible impact. Preserve intent New UX can change, safeguard cannot disappear.
Existing page contains a field not represented in the design Need to confirm whether field is obsolete, derived elsewhere, or still required. Clarify Open question before story is ready.
Typical design review

What I look for beyond the happy path

  • Empty states and first-time use
  • Role and permission differences
  • Error and validation behaviour
  • Existing data and backward compatibility
  • State transitions that are not obvious from static designs
  • Actions that depend on another area of the product
How it becomes delivery-ready

From design intent to testable behaviour

  • Functional requirements
  • User stories and acceptance criteria
  • Process or state flows where needed
  • Open questions and assumptions
  • UAT scenarios tied back to the requirement
Example acceptance criteria style

Given a user does not have permission to perform the action,
When the redesigned experience is loaded,
Then the restricted action is not available and the user cannot perform it through an alternate path.

Given an existing record contains data created under the legacy workflow,
When the user opens the redesigned experience,
Then the existing data remains correctly represented and does not require re-entry unless explicitly designed.

My contribution

I translate modernization and redesign work into behaviour the delivery team can implement safely. That includes comparing new designs with existing functionality, identifying missing rules or states, documenting decisions, writing development-ready stories and acceptance criteria, and carrying those decisions into UAT.

Case 04 · Migration + decommissioning
Project type Replacement of a background screening platform
Business journey Applicant screening / leasing process
Focus Current/future state, integration impacts, cutover, migration, decommissioning

Replacing a Background Check Platform

Replacing a vendor or tool is not just a procurement change. The applicant journey, product integration, status model, historical records, support process, and in-flight transactions can all depend on the platform being removed.

Current to future state
Area Current state Future state question
Screening request Application triggers request to legacy provider. What event triggers the new provider, and does the request payload change?
Status Legacy statuses appear in the applicant workflow. How do new provider statuses map to the product's existing language?
Results Results are returned through the existing integration. What data is returned, where is it stored, and who can see it?
History Past screenings may be available through the legacy tool. What must be migrated, retained elsewhere, or remain read-only?
Support Existing procedures reference the old platform. What documentation, access, and escalation paths change at cutover?
Cutover risk

In-flight applicants

One of the first migration questions I would surface is what happens to an applicant whose background check starts with the old provider but has not completed when the new platform goes live.

This needs an explicit cutover rule. Otherwise the business can end up with duplicate screening requests, missing results, or applications stuck between two providers.
Decommissioning checklist
  • Legacy API calls and credentials
  • Scheduled jobs or callbacks
  • Old UI links and configuration
  • Historical records and retention requirements
  • Reports that rely on legacy data
  • Support procedures and operational access
  • In-flight transactions at cutover
Representative status mapping
Legacy concept New provider concept Product-facing status Decision needed?
Pending Processing In progress No — equivalent meaning.
Completed Complete Complete No — verify result availability.
Error Failed / Unable to process Attention required Yes — define retry and support path.
No direct equivalent Additional review TBD Yes — business meaning must be agreed before mapping.
My contribution

My focus in this kind of project is continuity across the entire business journey. I map current dependencies, define the future-state workflow, identify migration and integration impacts, document cutover scenarios, and make sure the legacy platform can actually be retired without leaving hidden dependencies behind.

Case 05 · How I work today

AI's role Extraction, comparison, first drafts, edge-case prompts

How I Use AI in Requirements Work

I use AI to speed up the mechanical parts of analysis, but I do not treat a fluent draft as a correct requirement. The most useful output is often not the story itself — it is the assumptions and missing information I need to challenge before refinement.

My workflow
Stakeholder notes / design / ticket / existing behaviour
↓
AI extracts problem, actors, proposed behaviour, rules, assumptions, open questions
↓
I validate against product context and known business rules
↓
AI assists with first draft of stories / acceptance criteria / edge-case prompts
↓
I remove unsupported assumptions, add missing context, and confirm unresolved questions
↓
Refinement-ready story and supporting documentation
Worked example
Step Example
Raw note “Users should be able to cancel a booking. Obviously not once it has started. Refund handling is the same as today.”
AI draft Creates a cancellation story and assumes a refund is automatically issued.
What I reject The refund behaviour is an assumption. “Same as today” needs to be resolved against the existing process, which may have multiple refund paths.
Questions I surface What counts as “started”? Are there booking types with different cancellation rules? Who can cancel? What does the user see if refund processing fails?
Final direction Draft only after the existing cancellation and refund rules are confirmed, then write acceptance criteria around the verified behaviour.
Where AI helps me
  • Extracting decisions and open questions from notes
  • Comparing designs against existing requirements
  • Drafting user stories and acceptance criteria
  • Suggesting edge cases I may want to validate
  • Organising repetitive documentation
Where I do not outsource judgment
  • Business-rule validation
  • Scope and prioritization decisions
  • Product context and historical behaviour
  • Whether an assumption is safe to turn into a requirement
  • Final acceptance criteria and sign-off
How I use this today

I use this approach for story development, design-to-requirements translation, and delivery documentation. The value for me is not simply writing faster. It is getting assumptions, historical context, and unanswered questions in front of me earlier while keeping the final analysis grounded in the actual product.

How I work

A few things I consistently bring into the work.

My strongest work tends to sit between business context and delivery detail: enough product understanding to challenge the request, and enough technical detail to make the result usable by Engineering and QA.

I look for the rules behind the request.

A stakeholder usually describes the change they want, not every condition around it. I try to uncover those rules before the request becomes a story.

I bring edge cases in early.

Exceptions usually expose missing permissions, states, dependencies, or business rules that the happy path does not show.

I write for Product, Engineering, and QA together.

If three people can reasonably interpret the same story three different ways, I treat that as a requirement that still needs work.

I am comfortable working inside existing products.

Modernization, integrations, and migrations often require understanding what already exists before deciding what should change.
