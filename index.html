Case 01  /  2022 – 2025
Sector
Property technology, multifamily SaaS
Product
Greenfield AI renter engagement layer over a leasing CRM
Role
Senior BA, discovery through release
Reconstructed

An AI leasing assistant that knows when to stop

The hard requirement was never what the AI should answer. It was when the AI should stop and hand over.

Situation
Renter inquiries arrive 24/7 across channels
Constraint
Small leasing teams, office hours only
Cost
Out-of-hours inquiries go cold in the gap

The objective was an AI layer that could handle common renter inquiries while recognising when a leasing professional needed to step in. My first step was to define those boundaries: what the AI could handle on its own, when it should ask for more information, and when it had to hand off.

Prospect conversation journey

Artifact — user flow

Prospect initiates conversation
AI identifies intent
Collect required information
Enough information to act? No → ask follow-up, return to collection
Determine next action

One of

Answer question
Recommend next step
Escalate to agent
Capture interaction and context
Sync to CRM / leasing workflow

I treated escalation as part of the expected journey rather than as a failure state. That made the handoff conditions, context requirements, and fallback behaviour part of the requirements from the start.

Key measure Missed escalation rate AI-handled conversations subsequently determined to have required human intervention.
Key measure Containment rate Conversations resolved without human intervention.
Key measure Escalation completeness Escalations reaching an agent with conversation context and escalation reason intact.

These are representative measures I would use to evaluate the solution. I would prioritise missed escalations over containment: resolving more conversations automatically has limited value if the system occasionally leaves a prospect without the human help they needed.

Full artifacts — requirements, user story, edge cases, measures

Functional requirements

FR-01  Identify prospect intent

The system shall analyze each incoming prospect message and determine the applicable supported intent.

Supported intents:

  • Property inquiry
  • Availability
  • Pricing
  • Amenities
  • Viewing request
  • Application question
  • Existing tenant inquiry
  • Unsupported request
FR-02  Determine response confidence

The system shall assign a confidence level to each determined intent. Where confidence falls below the configured threshold, the conversation shall be treated as requiring human assistance regardless of intent type.

FR-03  Escalate with context

Where a conversation is routed for human follow-up, the system shall retain and present the full conversation context, the identified intent, and the reason for escalation to the receiving leasing professional.

FR-04  Record every interaction

The system shall record each prospect interaction against the prospect record, including AI-handled exchanges, regardless of whether escalation occurred.

User story and acceptance criteria

US-01  Prospect inquiry

As a prospective renter, I want to ask questions about a property, so that I can obtain information without waiting for a leasing agent.

Scenario 1 — Known informationGiven the prospect asks a question supported by available property information When the AI determines the prospect's intent with confidence at or above threshold Then the system provides the relevant response And the interaction is recorded against the prospect record.
Scenario 2 — Missing informationGiven additional information is required to answer When the AI cannot determine the appropriate response from what has been provided Then the prospect is prompted for the specific missing information And the conversation remains with the AI.
Scenario 3 — Low confidence or unsupportedGiven the request cannot be handled reliably When the system determines that human assistance is required Then the conversation is routed for human follow-up And the conversation context and escalation reason are retained And the prospect is told a person will follow up.

Edge cases raised during refinement

Edge case Defined behavior
Existing tenant uses the prospect channel Classified as existing-tenant inquiry; routed to the resident service path, not answered as leasing.
Question with fair-housing exposure Treated as unsupported regardless of confidence; escalated immediately with no AI-generated answer.
Same prospect across two channels Interactions attach to one prospect record; the agent sees one thread, not two.
Prospect abandons mid-collection Partial context retained; conversation resumable rather than restarted.
Prospect explicitly asks for a human Immediate escalation, no further AI questions.
No agent available Escalation queued and acknowledged; it does not silently fall back to the AI.

MVP prioritization

Capability Value Risk Decision Reasoning
Supported FAQ intents High Low MVP Highest volume, lowest consequence if imperfect
Clarification flow High Medium MVP Without it the assistant guesses, which is the failure mode we're avoiding
Human escalation Critical Medium MVP Made lower-confidence AI scenarios safe enough to release
Autonomous leasing actions High High Later Committing a prospect to anything needed a stronger trust record first
Advanced personalization Medium High Later Higher-risk behaviour that needed stronger evidence and controls before expanding
Representative prioritization. Human escalation was included in MVP because it provided a safe path for lower-confidence scenarios. Higher-risk autonomous behaviour was deferred until there was enough evidence to support it.

Measure Definition Why it matters

Containment rate Conversations resolved without human intervention Shows the operational value of the automation
Correct escalation rate Escalations an agent agreed genuinely needed a human Guards against simply escalating everything
First response time Prospect message to first substantive reply, in and out of hours Measures the original out-of-hours problem directly
Escalation completeness Escalations arriving with full history and stated reason Ensures the handoff does not create additional work for the agent

My contribution

I translated the AI product objective into explicit behavioural boundaries: supported intents, clarification paths, escalation conditions, context preservation, and testable acceptance criteria. I also used those boundaries to separate what was safe for MVP from capabilities that needed more evidence first.

Case 02  /  2016 – 2021
Sector
Enterprise SaaS, regulated housing
Scope
Conventional, affordable, tax credit, rent-stabilized, student, senior, military
Role
BA, requirements and delivery
Reconstructed

The one-line request with seven answers

A one-line request, seven program types, and a set of business rules that wasn't visible in the original ask.

Request
Change a date on a lease
Reality
Seven programs, seven rule sets, one engine
Risk
A conventional fix becomes a compliance finding

Taken literally, the request becomes a simple "change the date" story. The real analysis starts by asking which program governs the lease and what downstream rules the change affects — before a single story exists.

Date modification decision path

Artifact — decision tree

Lease status? Closed → blocked with explanation
Identify property program type

The branch that isn't in the request — program type — is the whole analysis.

Key measure Cross-program defect leakage Post-release defects affecting a program the change was not written for.
Key measure Rule coverage Scenarios in the business rules matrix with a corresponding test.
Key measure Refusal clarity Blocked attempts diagnosable from the message alone, without reproducing.

These are representative measures I would use for this type of change. Cross-program defects are particularly useful because they show whether impact analysis covered the wider product, not just the scenario that triggered the request.

Full artifacts — business rules matrix, requirements, AC, measures

Cross-program business rules matrix

Lease scenario Conventional Affordable / tax credit Student Expected behavior
Active lease Allow Validate certification Validate academic term Conditional — permitted where the program rule passes
Future lease Allow Validate against income limits in effect at move-in Validate term start Conditional — new date must precede move-in in all programs
Closed lease Block Block Block Uniformly blocked — state the reason on the record
Pending workflow Check dependency Check compliance in flight Check term dependency Conditional — in-flight approvals resolve before the change applies
Mid-certification period n/a Block or trigger recertification n/a Program-specific — no equivalent rule outside regulated programs
Representative rules, deliberately simplified. A scenario that is routine in one program can trigger additional compliance rules in another.

Impact analysis

Affected area Impact of a date change Analysis required before estimation
Charge schedule Scheduled charges recalculate; proration may change Which charge types are date-derived vs. fixed
Compliance certification Certification validity window may shift or void Whether the program treats the date as certification-relevant
Regulatory reporting Prior submitted periods may become inconsistent Whether any affected period has already been reported
In-flight workflows Pending approvals may be based on the old value Which workflows reference the date as an approval condition
Audit and history Prior state must remain reconstructible Whether refused attempts are also retained
This is the point where a seemingly small request becomes a properly scoped piece of work: changing one field can affect charges, compliance, reporting, in-flight workflows, and audit history.

Requirements derived from the matrix

FR-01  Apply program-specific validation

The system shall determine the property program type associated with the lease and apply the validation rule set defined for that program before permitting a date modification.

FR-02  Explain refusals

Where a modification is refused, the system shall identify the specific rule that was not satisfied. A generic failure message is not acceptable.

FR-03  Recalculate dependent values

Where a modification is applied, the system shall recalculate all values dependent on the modified date and present the resulting changes to the user before commit.

FR-04  Maintain an audit record

The system shall record prior value, new value, acting user, timestamp, and validation outcome for every attempted modification, including refused attempts.

Acceptance criteria

US-01  Modify a lease date

As a leasing administrator, I want to correct a lease-related date, so that the record reflects the agreement without breaching program rules.

Scenario 1 — Permitted changeGiven an active conventional lease When the administrator submits a new date within the lease term Then dependent charges are recalculated and shown for confirmation And the change is committed with an audit record on confirmation.
Scenario 2 — Blocked by program ruleGiven a lease under a regulated housing program When the submitted date would invalidate the current certification Then the change is refused And the specific compliance rule that failed is named in the message And the refused attempt is recorded in the audit trail.
Scenario 3 — Blocked by lease statusGiven a closed lease When the administrator attempts any date modification Then the action is unavailable And the reason is stated on the record rather than on submission failure.

Full measure set

Measure Definition Why it matters

Rule coverage Matrix scenarios with an automated or scripted test Confirms that documented rules are represented in testing
Refusal clarity Blocked attempts diagnosable from the message alone Reduces unnecessary support investigation
Requirement churn Stories reopened in-sprint for missing rule detail Shows whether key rules were identified before development

My contribution

I broke a small-looking product request into program-specific rules and downstream impacts before estimation. From there, I translated the rules into requirements and acceptance criteria so Engineering and QA did not have to infer conditional behaviour during delivery.

Case 03  /  2025 – present
Sector
Education technology, K–12 SaaS
Product
Learning management platform, quarterly releases
Role
Senior Business Analyst on the delivery team
Reconstructed

Making submission status mean one thing

The complaint was visibility. The cause was vocabulary — "submitted" and "late" meant different things in different parts of the product.

Symptom
Teachers can't tell who submitted or what's late
Cause
Each view derived its own status
Blocker
No view can be trusted until the terms are fixed

I started with the state model before discussing screen changes. The definitions needed to work across the perspectives of teachers, students, administrators, Product, Engineering, and QA — otherwise each view could continue interpreting status differently.

Submission state model

Artifact — state machine

Not started
In progress
Submitted Late = flag, not state
Returned
Resubmitted
Completed

Late is an attribute of the submission, not a state. That one decision removed the contradiction where a submitted-but-late item had to exist in two states at once.

Key measure Status agreement Teacher view and student view showing identical status for the same submission.
Key measure Unaided task completion UAT participants identifying non-submitters without help or drilling in.
Key measure Undefined transitions State changes recorded outside the permitted transitions in the model.

These are representative measures I would use for this type of feature. Status agreement comes first: if a teacher and student can see different answers for the same submission, the visibility problem has not really been solved.

Full artifacts — stakeholder translation, requirements, AC, UAT plan

Stakeholder needs, translated

Stakeholder Stated need What that required from the system
Teacher "I need to see who hasn't submitted" One status per student per assignment, filterable, with an action-required distinction
Student "I don't know if it went through" The same status the teacher sees, with no ambiguous in-between
Administration "Late has to mean the same thing everywhere" Lateness derived from one rule, not set per view
Product "It has to hold when we add assignment types" A state model independent of assignment type
Engineering "Tell us the actual states" Explicit states and permitted transitions, not adjectives
QA "How do I know it's right?" Each transition observable and independently testable

State transition table

Current state Event New state Late flag Action required
Not started Student begins work In progress No No
In progress Submit before deadline Submitted No Yes — teacher review
In progress Submit after deadline Submitted Yes — set once Yes — teacher review
In progress Deadline passes, no submission In progress Not applicable Yes — non-submitter
Submitted Teacher returns for revision Returned Retained No — with student
Returned Student submits again Resubmitted Retained from original Yes — teacher review
Submitted / Resubmitted Teacher marks complete Completed Retained No
Completed Any submission event Rejected — undefined transition, logged
Because lateness is stored separately from state, a returned and resubmitted item can retain its original submission history without creating contradictory statuses.

Functional requirements

FR-01  Single submission state

Each student assignment shall hold exactly one submission state at any time, drawn from the defined state set. Views shall render this state rather than deriving their own.

FR-02  Lateness as an attribute

Lateness shall be evaluated once at submission against the assignment due date and stored as an attribute of the submission. Lateness shall not alter the submission state.

FR-03  Action-required derivation

The system shall derive an action-required indicator for the teacher from submission state and shall not require the teacher to configure it.

FR-04  Transition integrity

The system shall permit only the transitions defined in the state model. Attempts at undefined transitions shall be rejected and logged.

Acceptance criteria

US-01  Assignment submission visibility

As a teacher, I want to see the submission status of every student for an assignment, so that I know who needs my attention without opening each one.

Scenario 1 — On-time submissionGiven a student submits before the due date When the teacher views the assignment roster Then the student shows as Submitted And no late indicator is shown And the student sees the same status.
Scenario 2 — Late submissionGiven a student submits after the due date When the teacher views the assignment roster Then the student shows as Submitted with a late indicator And the item appears under action required And lateness is not recalculated on subsequent views.
Scenario 3 — Returned and resubmittedGiven a submission has been returned for revision When the student resubmits Then the state becomes Resubmitted And the original late indicator is preserved And the item returns to the teacher's action-required list.

UAT approach

UAT scenario Participant Pass condition
Identify all non-submitters for one assignment Teacher Completed without opening individual submissions
Distinguish late from on-time at a glance Teacher Correct on every row, unprompted
Confirm own submission went through Student Status found without asking the teacher

My contribution

I reframed a visibility request as a definition problem, created a shared state and transition model, and carried that model through requirements, acceptance criteria, and UAT scenarios.

Process transformation with no system to point at

No backlog, no product, no obvious solution — just business initiatives and stakeholders who described the same process five different ways.

Starting point
Outcomes stated, current state undocumented
Open question
Whether technology was the answer at all
First job

Different stakeholders described different parts of the same process, and each version was only partially complete. I started by bringing those views together into one current-state process that everyone could review before we discussed solutions.

The value isn't simply reducing the number of steps. The future state removes duplicate entry, reduces manual reporting, and makes request status visible without relying on someone's inbox.

Key measure Intake completeness Requests accepted with all required information at first submission.

These are representative measures I would use for this type of engagement. I would start with current-state sign-off because solution design is difficult to trust if the business does not first agree on how the process works today.

Business requirements

BR-01  Structured intake

Requests shall be submitted through a defined intake format capturing all information required to begin work, with mandatory fields validated at submission.

BR-02  Visible status

The current status of any request shall be visible to the requester and the reviewing business unit without contacting an individual.

BR-03  Defined routing

Requests shall be routed to the responsible business unit according to documented rules based on request attributes, not individual discretion.

BR-04  Single record

Each request shall exist as one authoritative record. Re-entry of the same information into a second system shall not be required.

BR-05  Reporting from source

Operational reporting shall be generated from request records without manual assembly.

Case 05  /  Ongoing
Type
Own project, applied in current practice
Problem

Role

Own work

AI-assisted requirements, with the judgment left in

The failure mode of AI in analysis isn't bad writing. It's confident writing that's wrong in ways only someone who knows the product can see.

Temptation
Prompt it and paste the output
Failure mode
Assumptions arrive dressed as requirements
Design response
A workflow with explicit human gates

I use AI for extraction, first drafts, and edge-case prompts, but I keep validation with the BA. The biggest benefit for me isn't the first draft — it's surfacing missing information and unanswered questions before a story reaches refinement.

Both review points stay human. AI can accelerate the analysis, but assumptions still need to be challenged and confirmed before they become requirements.

Quality signal Untouched pass-through AI drafts accepted with no amendment. I treat a high rate here as a reason to check whether the review is deep enough.

I care more about whether this workflow catches missing information than how quickly it produces text. Drafting speed is useful, but only if the resulting story is also better prepared for refinement.

Full artifacts — worked example, governance rules, measures

Worked example

Stage Content
Raw stakeholder note "Users should be able to cancel a booking. Obviously not once it's started. Refund handling is the same as today."
AI draft "As a user, I want to cancel a booking so that I can free up my schedule. AC: Given a booking exists, when the user selects Cancel, then the booking is cancelled and a refund is issued per existing policy."
What I rejected The refund clause. "Same as today" had been converted into a requirement without confirming which existing refund path applied. The draft also treated any existing booking as cancellable even though the stakeholder had already mentioned an exclusion.
What I changed I separated cancellation and refund into different concerns, defined what "started" needed to mean, and added states the original note did not cover: already cancelled, in progress, completed, and cancellation attempted during a payment retry.
Sent back to the stakeholder Which refund path applies? Does "started" mean scheduled start or actual commencement? Who, if anyone, may cancel on another user's behalf?
Final requirement Cancellation permitted only from defined pre-commencement states, with "commenced" defined against actual start; refund treatment specified per path and confirmed by the business owner; delegated cancellation deferred to a separate story with its own permissions analysis.
Synthetic example. The point is the shape of the intervention, not the domain.

Governance rules the workflow is held to

G-01  Evidence attached

Any AI-produced statement about existing behavior must be traceable to the source material it came from, such as a ticket, comment, design file, or documented product behaviour.

G-02  Assumptions surface, never settle

Where the AI has inferred something, I keep it visible as an assumption or open question until it can be confirmed. It does not become acceptance criteria by default.

G-03  The BA owns the artifact

Nothing reaches refinement without being reviewed by me. I validate the business logic, remove unsupported assumptions, and add the product context the AI cannot reliably infer on its own.

G-04  Estimation is advisory

Where AI assists with sizing, it provides supporting reasoning and comparable work for the team to review. It does not replace team estimation or set the final number.

How I work

A few principles I bring into the work

Edge cases early

I try to identify exceptions while the requirement is still being shaped, not after development starts. They usually expose missing rules, dependencies, or states that the happy path doesn't show.

Find the rules behind the request

A stakeholder request often describes the desired change without describing all of the conditions around it. I spend time finding those rules before turning the request into stories.

Requirements everyone can use

I write requirements for Product, Engineering, and QA to work from together. If the same story is being interpreted three different ways, I treat that as a requirements problem that still needs resolving.

AI as an analysis tool

I use AI regularly for extraction, drafting, comparison, and finding possible gaps. I still validate the business logic myself and keep assumptions visible until a person can confirm them.
