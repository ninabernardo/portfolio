Case 01 · AI product
EnvironmentProperty technology / multifamily SaaS
My roleSenior Business Analyst supporting discovery through delivery
FocusAI renter engagement, leasing workflow, escalation behaviour

AI Leasing Assistant

The objective was an AI layer that could handle common renter inquiries while recognising when a leasing professional needed to step in. My first step was to define those boundaries.

The harder part was not teaching the AI what to answer. It was defining when it should stop trying.
What I needed to understand

Where could automation help without creating a bad renter experience?

Renter inquiries can arrive outside office hours, but not every conversation is safe or useful to automate.

My analysis

I treated escalation as part of the normal journey.

The requirements had to cover when escalation happens, what context follows the conversation, and what the prospect sees.

Simplified conversation flow
Prospect sends inquiry
↓
Identify intent + available context
↓
Supported intent → respond · Missing info → clarify · Unsupported / sensitive / human requested → escalate
View detailed analysis
Representative requirements
  • Identify likely intent before selecting a response path.
  • Low-confidence or unsupported requests must not be presented as confidently resolved.
  • A prospect asking for a person must be able to leave the automated path.
  • Relevant conversation context must be preserved during handoff.
Edge cases
  • Explicit request for a person
  • Fair-housing-sensitive question
  • Duplicate or multi-channel prospect
  • No agent available after escalation
MVP
  • Supported FAQ intents
  • Clarification for missing information
  • Human escalation with context
  • Interaction history
Deferred
  • Autonomous high-risk leasing actions
  • Deep personalization without controls
  • Behaviour depending on unverifiable data
My contribution

I translated an AI product objective into behavioural boundaries, conversation paths, escalation conditions, requirements, acceptance criteria, and MVP decisions.

Case 02 · Integration
EnvironmentEnterprise property-management SaaS
Product areaLeasing & Rents
FocusLease execution, e-signature integration, downstream status handling

E-Signature Integration for Leasing

The BA work is making the handoff between systems explicit — what gets sent, what comes back, what each status means, and what happens when the happy path breaks.

End-to-end workflow
Lease generated
↓
Validate document + signer data
↓
Create signature request → Resident signs → Status returns → Product updates lease
View detailed analysis
Discovery questions
  • Which system owns each piece of signer and lease data?
  • Who can initiate, void, or resend?
  • How are multiple signers handled?
  • What happens if the lease changes after send?
Representative field mapping
Lease / transaction IDReference / envelope metadata
Resident name / emailSigner information
Generated lease documentDocument to be signed
Exception scenario

What if the lease changes after it has already been sent?

The workflow needs an explicit rule for whether the existing request remains valid, whether it must be voided, and how the revised lease is resent.

My contribution

I documented the trigger, data mapping, statuses, permissions, failure paths, retries, and user-facing behaviour across systems.

Case 03 · Modernization
EnvironmentK–12 learning platform / SaaS
My roleSenior Business Analyst on the delivery team
FocusLegacy behaviour, redesign, requirements, acceptance criteria, UAT

Modernizing an Established LMS

My role is to help separate behaviour that must be preserved from behaviour that exists only because the old experience worked that way.

A redesigned screen can look simpler while accidentally removing behaviour the business still depends on.
How I approach it

I compare the existing product, intended design, and business rules.

I look for missing states, permissions, validations, dependencies, and actions before a design is treated as development-ready.

What I am trying to avoid

Rebuilding legacy behaviour blindly — or losing it accidentally.

Some behaviours are real requirements. Others are historical workarounds or UI limitations.

View detailed analysis
Modernization analysis
Existing behaviourDecisionRequirement impact
Action available only to certain usersPreserveInclude permission AC.
Multi-page legacy navigationRedesignConsolidate where possible.
Warning before destructive actionPreserve intentUX can change; safeguard cannot disappear.
Legacy field missing from designClarifyOpen question before ready.
My contribution

I compare new designs with existing functionality, identify missing rules or states, document decisions, write delivery-ready stories and acceptance criteria, and carry those decisions into UAT.

Case 04 · Migration + decommissioning
Project typeReplacement of a background screening platform
Business journeyApplicant screening / leasing process
FocusCurrent/future state, integration impacts, cutover, migration, decommissioning

Replacing a Background Check Platform

Replacing a vendor is not just a procurement change. The applicant journey, integration, status model, historical records, support process, and in-flight transactions can all depend on the platform being removed.

Cutover risk

In-flight applicants

What happens to an applicant whose screening starts with the old provider but has not completed when the new platform goes live?

Decommissioning

The old platform is not gone just because new requests stop.

Credentials, jobs, callbacks, history, reporting, support access, and in-flight work all need explicit exit criteria.

Screening requestLegacy providerWhat triggers the replacement provider?
StatusLegacy statusesHow do new statuses map to product language?
HistoryPast screenings in old platformWhat must be migrated, retained, or remain read-only?
SupportOld procedures and accessWhat changes at cutover?
My contribution

I map current dependencies, define the future-state workflow, identify migration and integration impacts, document cutover scenarios, and make sure the legacy platform can actually be retired.

Case 05 · AI-assisted BA workflow
ProblemRequirements reaching refinement with hidden assumptions, gaps, or incomplete context
ToolingClaude + existing product and delivery context
FocusRequirements readiness, story development, edge cases, assumptions, dependencies

BA Pre-Flight Workflow

I built a repeatable AI-assisted pre-flight step for requirements work before a story reaches refinement.

Before I ask whether a story is well written, I want to know whether we understand the problem well enough to write it.
Pre-flight workflow
Raw request / notes / design / existing ticket
↓
Extract goal + actors + stated rules
↓
Separate confirmed facts from assumptions
↓
Surface missing states, permissions, validations, dependencies and edge cases
↓
Open questions → story + AC from confirmed behaviour → BA review
View detailed analysis
What Claude helps with
  • Extracting stated requirements
  • Identifying possible assumptions
  • Suggesting edge cases worth validating
  • Structuring open questions
  • Drafting stories and AC after analysis
What stays with me
  • Determining whether assumptions are supported
  • Understanding historical behaviour
  • Resolving conflicting information
  • Scope and prioritization
  • Final requirement and AC
Worked example

Source note: “Users should be able to cancel a booking. Obviously not once it has started. Refund handling is the same as today.”

The workflow can still return “not ready.” That is useful if it tells me exactly what needs to be resolved next.
Why I built it

I use AI here as a quality and preparation layer, not as the owner of the requirement. The benefit is getting gaps, assumptions, and unanswered questions in front of me earlier.

Case 06 · AI-assisted estimation support
ProblemEstimation starts without enough historical or technical context
SourcesComparable Azure DevOps tickets + relevant code analysis
BoundaryAI provides evidence and context; Engineering still owns the estimate

Estimator Aide

I started building an AI-assisted estimation workflow that looks for comparable Azure DevOps work and relevant code areas before refinement.

The goal is not for AI to estimate the story. It is to give Engineering better evidence before they do.
Estimator Aide workflow
Proposed story / requirement
↓
Find comparable ADO tickets
↓
Review similar implementation + relevant code areas
↓
Summarize complexity signals + questions
↓
Engineering validates evidence and owns the final estimate
View detailed analysis
Useful estimation signals
  • Likely components touched
  • Shared versus isolated logic
  • Integration dependencies
  • Data-model or migration impact
  • Regression-sensitive workflows
What it does not do
  • Assign story points automatically
  • Treat historical tickets as exact equivalents
  • Override Engineering judgment
  • Present code guesses as confirmed architecture
Example estimation brief

Comparable work + likely affected areas + complexity signals + questions for Engineering. Estimate intentionally left unassigned.

Why this matters

The Estimator Aide connects requirements work with delivery history and technical context, while keeping feasibility, implementation approach, and estimation with Engineering.

How I work

A few things I consistently bring into the work.

My strongest work tends to sit between business context and delivery detail.

I look for the rules behind the request.

A stakeholder usually describes the change they want, not every condition around it.

I bring edge cases in early.

Exceptions usually expose missing permissions, states, dependencies, or business rules.

I write for Product, Engineering, and QA together.

If three people can reasonably interpret the same story three different ways, it still needs work.

I am comfortable working inside existing products.

Modernization, integrations, and migrations often require understanding what already exists before deciding what should change.
